Study Profile

Human papillomavirus (HPV) infection is the most common sexually transmitted infection worldwide and the primary cause of cervical cancer. Although over 90% of infections are cleared spontaneously by the immune system within one to two years, a subset of women develop persistent infections with high-risk HPV genotypes that drive progression to cervical intraepithelial neoplasia and invasive malignancy. The biological factors that distinguish persistent from transient infections remain poorly understood. The CHICO Study was established to address this critical gap by investigating the role of the cervical microbiome in HPV infection dynamics through a large-scale, prospective, multi-omics cohort.

The CHICO Study integrates a decade of retrospective epidemiological data from 226,240 women who underwent cervical HPV screening at a major tertiary hospital in Chengdu, China (2014–2023), with a prospective longitudinal cohort of 2,447 women, including 2,218 HPV-positive participants and 229 HPV-negative controls. This dual design connects population-scale HPV prevalence trends with individual-level microbiome and host molecular profiles, enabling the identification of microbial features associated with infection, persistence, and progression.

Analyses of the CHICO cohort demonstrate that HPV prevalence in Chengdu remained stable at approximately 18–22% across the study period, with high-risk genotypes — particularly HPV-52, HPV-16, and HPV-58 — predominating. Cervical microbiome profiling reveals that HPV-positive women exhibit higher microbial diversity and marked depletion of protective Lactobacillus species, while taxa such as Brucella, Gardnerella, and Sphingomonas are enriched. Distinct microbial signatures differentiate persistent from non-persistent infections and are associated with cervical cancer status. Age-related shifts in microbial composition further correlate with HPV persistence and malignant progression. Single-nucleus transcriptomics uncovers epithelial subpopulations with altered gene expression linked to microbial dysbiosis and oncogenic signaling, suggesting potential mechanisms through which microbial communities influence viral persistence and carcinogenesis.

While previous studies have largely focused on the vaginal microbiome using single-modality approaches, the CHICO Study specifically targets the cervical transformation zone — the anatomical site of HPV infection, viral integration, and malignant transformation. The study employs a multi-omics strategy encompassing 16S rRNA gene sequencing, metagenomic sequencing, and single-nucleus RNA sequencing. This integrated approach captures microbial community composition, functional potential, and host epithelial and immune cellular states, providing a mechanistic framework for understanding how cervical microbial dysbiosis contributes to viral persistence and carcinogenesis.

This platform provides interactive access to key datasets and analyses from the CHICO Study. Users can explore cervical microbiome composition stratified by HPV infection status, risk category, and persistence outcome, including single-taxon distribution comparisons and compositional summaries across user-selected grouping schemes. To our knowledge, the CHICO cohort represents the largest prospective study to date investigating the cervical microbiome in the context of HPV infection. Accompanying pages describe the study rationale, cohort design, multi-omics methods, and contributing investigators.

"This study establishes a comprehensive multi-omics landscape of HPV infection, demonstrating that cervical microbiome disruption is strongly associated with HPV persistence and cervical cancer progression. The findings highlight microbial and epithelial signatures as potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets for preventing HPV-related cervical malignancies."

How To Read The App

Editorial Structure

The About page summarizes the manuscript narrative (rationale, cohort, and major findings), the Data Visualization page provides an interactive subset of microbiome comparisons, and the Authors page maps the manuscript roster to institutions and correspondence notes.

Analysis Workflow

  • Select a display method (single-taxon distribution or Top-N stacked composition).
  • Choose taxonomic level, HPV grouping type, and one or more groups to compare.
  • Generate the figure and inspect either a non-parametric test summary (single taxon) or a composition table (Top-N mode).
